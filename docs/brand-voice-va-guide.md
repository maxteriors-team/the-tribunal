# Maxteriors Brand Voice — VA Guide & Automation Prompt

Last updated: 2026-09-09

Two audiences for this doc:
1. **VAs** answering texts, emails, and DMs — Sections 1–6.
2. **Automations / AI agents** in The Tribunal — Section 7 (copy-paste system prompt).

---

## 1. The voice in one line

**A confident local pro who talks like a neighbor, not a salesman.**
Plain words, real specifics, zero pressure. The work and the 200+ five-star reviews do the persuading.

We are usually **not** the cheapest bid. So the job of every message is to make someone feel
safe spending more — never to out-shout the cheap guy.

---

## 2. The six rules (memorize these)

1. **Write like you talk.** Short sentences, under 20 words. Contractions always.
2. **Specifics beat adjectives.** "10-year warranty on parts" > "premium quality."
3. **Never dodge price.** Give the range confidently. Dodging looks like hiding.
4. **No pressure, ever.** Offer an easy out in the message. Calm sells; chasing repels.
5. **One question per message.** Make replying take five seconds.
6. **Sound like a person.** Sign "— Max" or your first name. Never "The Maxteriors Team."

---

## 3. Before you hit send — the 5-second check

- [ ] Would I say this out loud in someone's driveway?
- [ ] Is there exactly one question?
- [ ] Did I use a real number or a real detail?
- [ ] Zero hype words? (see banned list)
- [ ] One exclamation point max — ideally zero?
- [ ] Under 4 sentences for SMS, under 120 words for email?

If any box fails, cut words. Cutting almost always fixes it.

---

## 4. Word list

**Use:** roofline · track · one day · permanent · year-round · your color · we own the lights ·
warranty · neighbors · no pressure · real number · swing by · take a look

**Never use:** cutting-edge · transform · elevate · unleash · solutions · act now · limited time ·
luxury · world-class · premium · state-of-the-art · exciting news · hurry · don't miss out

**Never use these formats:** ALL CAPS · multiple !! · emoji in sales copy · "Hi [FIRST NAME]!!"

### Banned follow-up filler — never send a message that opens with these

"Just following up" · "Just checking in" · "Circling back" · "Touching base" · "Bumping this up" ·
"Wanted to reach out" · "Did you get my last message?" · "Per my last message" ·
"Hope this finds you well" · "Any thoughts on this?" · "Still interested?"

**Why:** every one of them announces that the message has no new information in it. It asks the
customer to do the work of remembering, and it reads as chasing — which is exactly the thing that
makes a higher-priced bid look desperate.

**The rule that replaces them:** *every follow-up carries something new.* A timeline, a season, a
price that hasn't moved, an opening on the calendar, a nearby address they can drive by. If you
have nothing new to say, don't send the message — or send the closing line and stop.

| Instead of | Send |
|---|---|
| "Just following up on my quote!" | "Prices haven't moved since March, and we've got two openings the week of the 14th." |
| "Just checking in — still interested?" | "We're about three weeks out now, so mid-October is the last comfortable install before the holidays." |
| "Did you get my last message?" | "Wanted you to have the number in writing: $4,200 for the full roofline, 10-year parts warranty." |
| "Any thoughts?" | "We finished a house on Lahser last week — happy to send the address if you want to see it lit." |

## 5. Copy-paste response library

Fill the `{brackets}`. Change words to fit — keep the shape and the calm.

### New lead comes in (send within 5 minutes)
> Hi {name} — Max with Maxteriors. Saw you're looking at {permanent lighting / Christmas lighting}
> for the house on {street}. Want me to swing by this week and get you a real number?
> Takes about 20 minutes.

### They ask "how much?" before an appointment
> Fair question. Most permanent lighting projects land between $3,000 and $5,000 depending on
> roofline length. I'd rather give you an exact number than a guess — can I take a look this week?

### Christmas lighting price question
> Full-service, so that's design, install, maintenance all season, takedown, and storage.
> And we own the lights — you're not stuck with a garage full of strands next year.
> Starts at $1,250. Want me to take a look at your place?

### No answer, second touch (wait 2 days) — lead with the new fact, not the follow-up
> No worries if the timing's off. Most people asking in {month} want it up before {the holidays /
> spring}, and we're running about {X} weeks out. Want me to save you a spot?

### No answer, third touch (wait 1 week — then stop)
> I'll get out of your inbox. If you want that number down the road, just text this line and
> I'll pick right back up. — Max

### They got a cheaper bid
> Totally fair — we're usually not the cheapest bid on the table. The difference is the track's
> aluminum, we mount it under the drip edge so you don't see it in daylight, and parts are covered
> for 10 years. If something fails in year six, we're still the ones fixing it.
> Happy to send a couple of local addresses you can drive by.

### They ask about financing (permanent lighting only)
> Yep. We run 24-month 0% through GreenSky, so a {$4,000} project is about {$167} a month with
> nothing extra. Or if you'd rather pay cash there's a discount — half down, half at completion.
> Want me to send the application link?

### They ask for financing on Christmas lighting
> Christmas lighting we keep simple — no financing on that one. It's {$X} with the deposit up front.
> Permanent lighting is the one we finance, if that's ever on the table.

### Appointment confirmation
> You're set for {day} at {time}. I'll text when I'm on the way. If something comes up,
> just reply here — easy to move.

### After install, review request
> Hey {name} — hope the lights are looking good. If you've got 30 seconds, a quick Google review
> helps us more than any ad we run. Either way, thanks for having us out. — Max

### Old unsold quote, seasonal revival
> Hi {name} — Max with Maxteriors. We quoted your roofline back in {month}. Prices haven't moved
> and we've got {season} openings if it's back on your radar. Want me to re-send the number?

### Complaint or something broke
> That shouldn't be happening — sorry. Let's get it handled. What are you seeing, and when's a good
> day this week for us to come out?
>
> (Then escalate to Max immediately. Do not promise a specific fix, credit, or timeline.)

## 6. Escalate to Max — do not answer these yourself

- Any custom quote or number outside the published ranges
- Christmas lighting deposit terms (rule not finalized yet)
- Warranty claims, refunds, credits, or anything a customer is upset about
- Requests to price-match a competitor
- Commercial / HOA / multi-property jobs
- Anything where you'd have to guess

Holding pattern while you wait on Max:
> Good question — let me get you a straight answer instead of a guess. I'll come back to you {today / tomorrow}.

## 7. Automation system prompt (copy-paste)

Paste this into any AI SMS/email automation in The Tribunal, above the task-specific instructions.

```
You write on behalf of Maxteriors, an outdoor lighting company in metro Detroit.

VOICE
Write like a confident local pro talking to a neighbor — never like a salesperson or a brand.
Plain words. Short sentences under 20 words. Contractions always. Calm, warm, never eager.
We are usually not the cheapest bid, so build trust by being specific and de-risking the purchase
(warranty, we own the lights, local addresses to drive by). Never out-shout a competitor on hype.

HARD RULES
- SMS: 1-3 sentences. Email: under 120 words.
- Exactly one question per message, and make it easy to answer.
- Never dodge price. Give the real range.
- Zero pressure. No urgency, no scarcity, no "act now."
- Max one exclamation point per message; prefer zero. No emoji in sales copy. No ALL CAPS.
- Sign as "— Max" when a signature is needed. Never "The Maxteriors Team."
- Never invent a price, date, timeline, warranty term, or promise not listed in FACTS.

BANNED WORDS
cutting-edge, transform, elevate, unleash, solutions, act now, limited time, luxury, world-class,
premium, state-of-the-art, exciting news, hurry, don't miss out.

BANNED FOLLOW-UP FILLER — never write these phrases in any message
"just following up", "just checking in", "circling back", "touching base", "bumping this",
"wanted to reach out", "did you get my last message", "per my last message",
"hope this finds you well", "any thoughts", "still interested".
Every follow-up must open with new information instead: a lead time, a season, a price that hasn't
changed, a calendar opening, or a nearby completed job. If there is no new information to give,
do not send a follow-up.

FACTS YOU MAY STATE
- Permanent lighting: app-controlled color-changing roofline system. Most projects $3,000-$5,000.
  50% deposit. 10-year parts warranty, 1-year labor.
  Financing: GreenSky 24-month 0% APR. Cash option: discount, half down and half at completion.
- Christmas lighting: full service — design, install, season-long maintenance, takedown, storage.
  Maxteriors owns the lights. $1,250 minimum. No financing available.
- Proof points: 200+ five-star reviews, local metro Detroit company, established.

ESCALATE — do not answer, hand to a human
Custom quotes outside the ranges above, Christmas lighting deposit terms, warranty claims, refunds,
upset customers, price-match requests, commercial/HOA jobs, anything requiring a guess.
When escalating, reply only: "Good question — let me get you a straight answer instead of a guess.
I'll come back to you today."
```

## 8. Quick QA rubric (for reviewing VA messages)

Score each message pass/fail. Three fails in a week = retrain on Sections 2 and 3.

| Check | Pass looks like |
|---|---|
| Sounds human | You could imagine Max saying it out loud |
| Length | SMS under 4 sentences, email under 120 words |
| One question | Exactly one, easy to answer |
| Specificity | At least one real number or concrete detail |
| No hype | Zero banned words |
| No pressure | Customer has an easy out |
| Follow-ups | Opens with new information — zero filler phrases from Section 4 |
| Accuracy | Every fact matches Section 7 FACTS |
